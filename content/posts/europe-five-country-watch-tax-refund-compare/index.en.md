If your European itinerary crosses several of Switzerland, Germany, France, Italy, and the UK, the same watch can end up costing you more than a full percentage point apart depending on where you buy it, once the refund is factored in. I've previously compared Switzerland, Germany, and France; this time Italy and the UK join the comparison — and the UK carries a major change many people still haven't caught up with. The short version first: **the UK now essentially offers no tax refund for tourists at all.** Details below.

Comparing European watch tax refunds means lining up the VAT rate, the refund threshold, and the actual claims process across Switzerland, Germany, France, Italy, and the UK, to work out which country leaves you with the lowest final price on the same watch. Looking at the tax rate alone is an easy way to get it wrong (for instance, assuming the UK still refunds tourists) — only by combining the rate, the threshold, and whether a refund is even possible can you calculate the real difference in the final price, and avoid overpaying by a percentage point or more without realizing it. It addresses the mistake most common in cross-border watch shopping: applying one country's rules to another, especially treating post-Brexit London as if it still worked the way it did a decade ago.

## VAT Rates at a Glance: Italy Highest, Switzerland Lowest

The standard VAT rates across the five destinations are roughly: France 20%, UK 20%, Italy 22%, Germany 19%, Switzerland 8.1% (Switzerland isn't in the EU, so its tax system is entirely independent). Looking at the rate alone, Italy is the "priciest" and Switzerland the "cheapest" — but a tax refund is never just about the rate number; the threshold, how much you actually get back, and how smooth the process is all matter just as much.

## Refund Thresholds: Four Countries Offer One, One No Longer Does

- **France:** Roughly €100.01 per invoice to qualify — a moderate threshold.
- **Germany:** Roughly €50 to qualify — a lower threshold, easier to trigger eligibility.
- **Italy:** Roughly €70.01 to qualify — between France and Germany.
- **Switzerland:** Roughly CHF 300 to qualify, and Switzerland's refund system operates entirely independently of the EU framework.
- **UK:** Not applicable — see the next section for why.

Specific thresholds and exchange rates shift over time; before buying, it's worth confirming the current figures directly with the store or the refund operator. What's given here is only a common range, not official data.

## The UK: Tourist Tax-Free Shopping Ended in 2021

This is the single most important thing to know upfront in this whole article. The UK abolished its tourist Retail Export Scheme on January 1, 2021. That means no matter which boutique in London you buy from, if you're carrying the watch out yourself in your luggage, **there is, in principle, no longer any way to claim a refund at the airport the way you could before Brexit.**

- **Field tip:** The "tax-free" option still available today mostly comes down to a small number of stores offering "direct overseas shipping" — you place the order in-store, and the store ships the item directly to an address outside the EU, rather than you carrying it out yourself; only in that scenario might UK VAT be excluded. If you're planning to buy a watch in the UK, confirm explicitly with the store beforehand whether it's a "refund" or "duty-free direct shipping" — the process and eligibility conditions for the two are completely different. A lot of people are still planning their London shopping trip around ten-year-old assumptions, only to discover at the airport that there's no refund counter to speak of anymore — this has tripped up plenty of people in recent years.

## Export Stamping: Switzerland and the UK Each Run Their Own System — Don't Mix Them Into a Schengen Itinerary

France, Germany, and Italy are all within the Schengen Area. If your itinerary is "buy in France → travel overland into Italy → fly out of the Schengen Area from an Italian airport," the refund form should, in principle, be stamped at **the point where you leave the Schengen Area** — not at just any airport along the way.

Switzerland and the UK are both outside the Schengen Area, and each needs to be handled separately, logically speaking:

- For a watch bought in Switzerland, you go through Switzerland's own customs process, independent of whichever Schengen countries you pass through afterward.
- For a watch bought in the UK, as covered above, there's generally no refund step to speak of anymore, so the question of "where to get it stamped" doesn't really apply.

**Key signal:** Whenever an itinerary combines "shopping in a Schengen country + Switzerland + UK," confirm the rules separately for each country — don't apply one country's process to the other two.

## The Final Price Is What Matters — Don't Let the Tax Rate Number Mislead You

Taken together — rate, threshold, whether a refund is possible, and how smooth the process is — Italy has the highest rate but mature refund infrastructure and a moderate threshold, so the effective refund percentage usually isn't bad; Switzerland has the lowest rate but a higher threshold and an independent system; the UK's rate isn't low, and a refund is largely off the table — if you're still counting on getting money back in London when planning your trip, it's worth redoing the math.
